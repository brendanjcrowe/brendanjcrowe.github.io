I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the University of Colorado Boulder, advised by Prof. Christoffer Heckman in the Autonomous Robotics and Perception Group (ARPG). I grew up in suburban Massachusetts, received a B.S. in Statistics from the University of New Hampshire, and spent two years as a machine learning engineer in industry before starting my Ph.D.

My research is on robotic perception and world representations: using generative models to complete and predict 3D scenes from partial observations, and sensing beyond cameras with lidar and mmWave radar. I believe that for robotics to see breakthroughs like those in NLP, we need robot-centric foundation models built on richer sensing and well-understood world representations.

Outside of work I enjoy skiing, hiking, and playing video games. I am an avid "reader" which means listening to audio books during my commute and while working out. Fantasy, Sci-Fi and classic literature make up the majority of what I read with the occasional history or culinary book in between.
